Colorful Semantics is a structured approach developed in speech and language therapy. This studio is an introductory practice tool, not official training.
Lesson 1 · The big idea

A sentence becomes easier when you can see what each part is doing.

Colorful Semantics separates a sentence into meaningful roles. Instead of asking a learner to “write a better sentence,” you can make the invisible structure visible—one question and one color at a time.

Lesson 2 · The hinge

What is happening?

Yellow marks the action or verb phrase: runs, is opening, will build. Once the action is clear, it becomes much easier to ask who is doing it and what, where, or when belongs with it.

It gives the sentence energy.
Without an action, learners may only list people and things.
It carries grammar.
Tense, agreement, and auxiliary verbs often live inside the yellow part.
It gives you a concrete prompt.
“What is happening?” is more usable than “Add a verb.”

Lesson 4 · Teacher lab

Prompt less. Reveal more.

Pick a classroom moment. The app gives you a short teaching sequence that moves from meaning to a complete sentence.

    Quick check

    A student says: “Boy… ball.” What is the most useful first move?

    Choose one response.

    Your five-minute starting routine

    1. Show one clear picture.
    2. Ask: “Who?” Place the orange chunk.
    3. Ask: “What is happening?” Place the yellow chunk.
    4. Say and tap the two-part sentence together.
    5. Add only one more color when the meaning is secure.

    The goal is not to use every color every time. The goal is to help the learner notice and control sentence meaning.

    Classroom mode

    Turn one idea into a teachable sentence

    Choose a familiar example or enter the meaning chunks yourself. Then reveal, remove, and rebuild the sentence with students.

    Leave a role blank when the sentence does not need it. A complete idea does not need every color.

    Teacher view5 meaning chunks
    Read the whole sentence once for meaning.

    What the sequence is doing

    You are reducing the language load without reducing the idea. The questions direct attention to meaning; the colors hold the sentence structure in working memory; the gradual removal checks whether the learner can take control without the visual support.
